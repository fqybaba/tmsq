## Synthesis (as returned)

**Verdict**

**(1) Same finding?** No source I opened reports it. The premise is already known. TMS (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html) says "ReLU makes negative interference free in the 1-sparse case… using a negative bias can convert small positive interferences into essentially being negative interferences". It also says pentagons sit off the unit circle because models set "a slight negative bias to cut off noise", driven by "the amount of positive interference". Chen et al. 2023, Table A.1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06301v1), gives b*=0 for the 4-gon, −0.28230 for the 5-gon and −0.61814 for the 6-gon. The 4-gon is the m=2 square of two orthogonal antipodal pairs, which is a zero-interference limit, not the mixed regime in the claim. Tlaie (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kbwHXpymfLkZpKExd) writes that "the bias vector is not negative until larger sparsities (when the antipodal structure is lost)". None of these perturbs the weights. The consequence, that antipodal features are the most fragile under weight RTN or noise and that crowding protects features, is not reported anywhere I checked.

**(2) Closest work:** The "crowding = fragile" results are all for input attacks and measured per model. TMS: vulnerability "sharply increases as superposition forms". Stevinson et al. (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11709v1): robust accuracy "decreases monotonically with superposition pressure". Gorton & Lewis (https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17456v2) report "one striking dip corresponding to antipodal superposition" and give no explanation. So for input attacks, antipodal models are less vulnerable. Your result is about a different perturbation space; it complements these papers rather than reversing them. Duan, "How Quantization Changes Interpretable Features" (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03002v1), measures per-feature RTN damage but has no bias or geometry analysis and leaves biases unquantized.

**(3) Suggested sentence:** "It is known that antipodal features need no negative bias while crowded polytopes learn increasingly negative ones (Elhage et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023). To our knowledge, this work is the first to show that this makes antipodal features the most fragile under post-training weight quantization and matched weight noise, so that more crowded features are damaged less. Prior robustness results linking superposition to fragility concern input-space adversarial attacks at the model level."

I opened only the sources cited above, so the other candidates in the list are not verified.

## Candidates (as returned, deduplicated by URL)

### Toy Models of Superposition (mechanism: negative bias vs. positive interference; antipodal pairs)
- Authors/year: Elhage, Hume, Olsson, Schiefer, Henighan, ... Olah (Anthropic), 2022
- URL: https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html
- Overlap (agent's label): closely related; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): States the mechanism the claim relies on, but never perturbs weights. Under ReLU, negative interference is free in the 1-sparse case, and a negative bias turns small positive interference into effectively negative interference. Pentagon features sit off the unit circle because the model sets 'a slight negative bias to cut off noise' with ||W_i|| = 1/(1-b_i), and this distance is 'primarily driven by the amount of positive interference'. So bias size tracks positive interference, which an antipodal pair (D = 1/2) does not have. The paper has no per-feature robustness result.
- Quote (agent): "Another interesting property is that ReLU makes negative interference free in the 1-sparse case. This explains why the solutions we've seen prefer to only have negative interference when possible. Further, using a negative bias can convert small positive interferences into essentially being negative interferences."

### Adversarial Examples Are Not Bugs, They Are Superposition
- Authors/year: Gorton & Lewis, 2025 (arXiv 2508.17456)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17456v2
- Overlap (agent's label): closely related; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): Uses the TMS model (uniform importance, density swept from 1.0 to 0.1). Model-level L2 adversarial vulnerability rises with features per dimension, with 'one striking dip corresponding to antipodal superposition'. So under INPUT perturbations, models in the antipodal regime are LESS vulnerable, the opposite direction to the claim for WEIGHT perturbations. It also says models offset interference with negative biases that an attack can overwhelm. Everything is model-level; there is no per-feature or weight-noise analysis. The authors flag the antipodal dip as unexplained.
- Quote (agent): "Firstly, as discussed earlier, we note a drop in vulnerability to adversarial examples when models switch to antipodal superposition."

### Adversarial Attacks Leverage Interference Between Features in Superposition
- Authors/year: Stevinson, Prieto, Barsbey & Birdal, 2025 (arXiv 2510.11709)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11709v1
- Overlap (agent's label): closely related; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): In controlled toy models (mainly a CE classifier without ReLU or biases, checked against an MSE setup with ReLU and biases) and in a ViT with a bottleneck, robust accuracy falls monotonically as superposition pressure rises. Attacks spend their budget on superposed features and leave an orthogonally represented class untouched. For input-space attacks, 'more superposition = more fragile', the opposite of the claim's per-feature direction for weight perturbations. It does not address weight noise, quantization or the bias mechanism.
- Quote (agent): "Robust accuracy decreases monotonically with superposition pressure [...] demonstrating that vulnerability scales with interference degree."

### How Quantization Changes Interpretable Features: A Sparse Autoencoder Analysis of Language Models (arXiv v1 title; the project notes cite it under another title)
- Authors/year: Duan, 2026 (arXiv 2606.03002)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.03002v1
- Overlap (agent's label): closely related; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): Per-feature survival of SAE features under RTN PTQ (INT8 to INT4) in Pythia-70M and Gemma-2-2B, measured as the Pearson correlation between FP and quantized activations. Survival is predictable from FP statistics (AUC 0.92-0.97), peak activation is the strongest marginal predictor, and rarer features are not more fragile once activation statistics are controlled. Its only mechanism is a 'signal vs. perturbation floor' heuristic. It measures no geometry, bias or superposition quantity, and it leaves biases unquantized.
- Quote (agent): "Mechanistically, quantization perturbations compete with each feature’s activation signal: high-signal features remain above the perturbation floor, while weak-signal features are more easily blurred."

### Feature Superposition in Neural Networks: From Theory to Practice (survey)
- Authors/year: Shi, Li, Han & Hernández-Lobato, 2026 (arXiv 2609.06862)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06862v1
- Overlap (agent's label): background; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): A survey. It says antipodal pairs and simplices exploit the ReLU sign structure (negative pre-activations on inactive coordinates are removed), and that a negative bias additionally removes small positive cross-talk at the cost of shrinking active signals. ### From Data Statistics to Feature Geometry: How Correlations Shape Superposition
- Authors/year: Prieto, Stevinson, Barsbey, Birdal & Mediano, 2026 (ICLR 2026; arXiv 2603.09972)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09972v1
- Overlap (agent's label): background; search angle: tms-robustness
- What it shows (agent): In TMS-style ReLU autoencoders (synthetic and bag-of-words data), sparse weakly correlated features need ReLU plus a negative bias to filter interference.
